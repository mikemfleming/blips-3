About Blips

Blips turns a living pattern of cells into music. Draw something, scatter some randomness, or start with a few words, then listen as the pattern changes.

A simple game of life

Conway’s Game of Life is a set of rules, rather than a game you win. Every cell has eight neighbors. A living cell survives with two or three living neighbors; otherwise it dies. An empty cell becomes alive with exactly three living neighbors. All cells change together each generation, creating patterns that settle, pulse, travel, or disappear. The grid wraps at its edges.

How the cells become music

Behind the fine cells is an invisible grid of 32 columns and 32 pitch rows. The rainbow scanner reads one column at a time, from left to right. Living cells in each region sound a note: higher rows make higher notes, lower rows make lower notes, and more living cells make the note louder, up to a limit. The sound moves from left to right with the scanner.

Slow and Fast change both the scanner’s pace and how quickly life evolves. The scanner and Conway run independently at roughly the same interval, so the pattern changes throughout a sweep. Leaving the page pauses the composition; press Play when you return.

Stabby or Smooth

Stabby plays a separate, short note at each scanner step. Smooth connects notes across neighboring active regions in the same pitch row, gently changing their volume and fading when the scanner reaches an empty region. Smooth is the default.

Draw while you listen

Select the pencil, then drag your mouse or finger across the grid. Starting a stroke on an empty cell draws; starting on a living cell erases. The brush is three cells wide and works while playing or paused. Drawn cells stay alive for 16 generations before returning to the usual rules. Random scatters fresh cells; the trash button clears the grid. Press Space or use Play to start and pause.

Start with a message

Add ?text=Hello+world to the page address to plant centered words as cells. Use + for spaces and %0A for a new line. Letters near the end stay protected longer than letters at the beginning, letting the message gradually unravel.

Try a message with a little randomness: ?text=Hello+world&density=10 adds a random background with a 10% chance of life per cell. Copy the full address to share your starting message and settings; each visit gets a fresh random background.
